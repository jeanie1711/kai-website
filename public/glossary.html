AI & automation

AI agent

An AI agent is software that uses an AI model to carry out steps of a task on its own, such as searching documents, drafting a reply or creating a task, rather than only answering a single prompt. Because it can read and act on company data, an AI agent needs its own identity, limited permissions and a named owner, just like a member of staff.

Workflow automation

Workflow automation uses software to run the routine steps of a business process automatically, such as routing a document, assigning a task or sending a reminder. Combined with AI, it can also handle steps that involve reading or writing text, while people keep the decisions that matter.

Human-in-the-loop

Human-in-the-loop means a named person reviews or approves certain steps of an AI workflow before it continues, for example confirming an unclear task owner or approving an invoice. In KAI workflows, sensitive decisions such as payments and system access always stay with people.

Defined actions

Defined actions are the specific things an AI agent is allowed to do, such as read a CRM record, update a task or send an approved email. Anything not on the list is blocked. Together with approved data and human oversight, defined actions are how KAI applies Zero Trust to AI agents.

Approved data sources

Approved data sources are the specific documents, folders, inboxes or systems an AI workflow is allowed to use, agreed in advance with a named owner for each. Anything not on the list, such as HR records or finance files, stays outside the workflow unless the business decides otherwise.

Sales Outreach (AI workflow)

Sales Outreach is an AI workflow in which AI agents verify and qualify leads from a CRM, sign-up forms or prospect lists, then suggest an angle, channel and tailored email or DM for each lead. A person approves the content and recipients before automation sends it, logs replies and updates the CRM.

Lead nurturing

Lead nurturing means keeping in regular, useful contact with leads who are not ready to buy yet, so they remember you when they are. In a KAI workflow, AI drafts content for an audience segment, a person reviews it, and automation sends it and tracks engagement.

See the Lead Nurturing workflow →

Knowledge Assistant

A Knowledge Assistant is an AI agent that answers employees' everyday questions using only approved company documents, such as policies, SOPs and sales materials, shows the source of each answer and follows each employee's existing access. Questions it cannot answer reliably are flagged to the document owner.

See the Knowledge Assistant workflow →

Pilot (AI workflow)

A pilot is a small, time-boxed first version of an AI workflow, tested with real users on one process. KAI agrees how success will be measured before building, such as time saved or errors avoided, then reviews the results before deciding what to expand.

Zero Trust & security

Zero Trust

Zero Trust is a security approach that never assumes a person, device or AI tool should have access just because it had access before. Every request to reach information is checked against clear rules each time, based on who is asking, from which device and for what.

See the Zero Trust Foundation →

Least privilege

Least privilege means giving each person, system or AI agent only the access it needs to do its job, and nothing more. Applied to AI, it means each agent gets its own identity with permissions limited to the agreed data and actions, reviewed as the workflow changes.

Oversharing

Oversharing is when files or folders are open to more people than need them, for example a folder shared with the whole company or with anyone who has the link. It matters more once AI tools are connected, because an AI assistant can surface overshared content in seconds.

Data classification

Data classification means grouping information by how sensitive it is, for example Internal, Confidential and Highly confidential, and attaching rules to each group about who can open it, on which devices and whether it can be shared. Protection can then travel with the file wherever it goes.

KAI Zero Trust Security Foundation

The KAI Zero Trust Security Foundation is KAI's paid security service for Microsoft 365. KAI reviews what a business already has, defines policies that fit how it works, sets them up in Microsoft 365 and tests the changes, and helps the team understand and use the new rules through Team Adoption (ACM). It builds on the KAI Zero Trust Baseline Policies, can be bought on its own and is recommended alongside KAI AI Workflows.

Policy Design

Policy Design is the first pillar of the KAI Zero Trust Security Foundation. KAI defines the policy intent, guardrails, responsibilities and governance an organisation needs across identity, access, device trust, data handling, AI usage and monitoring, aligned to how the business actually operates rather than copied from a template.

Technical Implementation

Technical Implementation is the second pillar of the KAI Zero Trust Security Foundation. KAI configures the agreed controls in Microsoft 365 and Entra for sign-in, access, devices and data, so the policies are enforced in the systems people use every day.

Team Adoption

Team Adoption is the third pillar of the KAI Zero Trust Security Foundation, delivered through ACM. KAI makes sure people understand and apply new expectations through stakeholder communication, leadership alignment, role-based training, Change Champions, rollout support and reinforcement. The engagement is complete only when the organisation can operate safely and consistently.

ACM (Adoption & Change Management)

Adoption & Change Management (ACM) is KAI's continuous people-and-behaviour discipline that turns new security controls, processes, AI workflows and technologies into sustained ways of working. Through communication, leadership alignment, role-based training, Change Champions and reinforcement, ACM bridges technical implementation and real behaviour.

KAI Zero Trust Security Pack

The KAI Zero Trust Security Pack is the consolidated deliverable a client receives from a Zero Trust Security Foundation engagement. It contains the designed policies, implementation evidence, operating guidance and a security maturity roadmap, giving the organisation a documented foundation it owns and can keep building on.

KAI OS

KAI OS

KAI OS is KAI's customer-led, technology-agnostic operating architecture and delivery methodology. It connects five layers (Customer Value Chain, Business Engines, AI & Automation Platform, Knowledge Base and Zero Trust Security Foundation), bound together by ACM as a continuous thread. KAI OS is an operating architecture and methodology today, not a finished software platform.

Technology-agnostic

In KAI OS, technology-agnostic means no vendor, platform or stack is mandatory at the architecture level. Tools are chosen to fit the solution, whether commercial or open-source, cloud or on-premise, never the solution forced to fit a product.

Solution-led

Solution-led means KAI defines the business outcome and designs the operating approach before selecting any technology. KAI clarifies the problem, the required capabilities and the right sequence first, which keeps every engagement focused on an outcome rather than a tool purchase.

Customer Value Chain

The Customer Value Chain is KAI's eight-stage customer journey (Awareness, Consideration, Purchase, Onboarding, Delivery, Support, Retention and Partnership) describing the value created at each stage. It is Layer 01 of KAI OS, where strategic intent and customer outcomes drive the design of the whole operating system.

Business Engines

Business Engines are structured, end-to-end operating processes designed to run consistently and measurably. KAI OS defines four interconnected Engines (Growth, Delivery, Product, and Hire & Train) that exchange inputs and feedback rather than running in a rigid sequence. They form Layer 02 of KAI OS.

Knowledge Base

The Knowledge Base is the governed source of truth and learning loop within KAI OS, forming Layer 04. It captures, structures and continuously updates SOPs, policies, decisions and institutional knowledge, the reliable foundation that responsible AI and automation depend on.
